# First lesson: today, in 25 minutes

**Prepare (2 min):** open [the course in English](https://humbertogaliza.github.io/kids-for-dev/?lang=en) on a computer or tablet with a keyboard. The first Python download may take a few seconds. Have paper ready. An adult controls any publication.

1. **Discover (5 min):** “What should we tell the computer to say?” Open Quest 1. Read the explanation and worked example; ask the child to point to `print`, parentheses, and quotes.
2. **Blocks (6 min):** ask the child to build `print("Moon")`. They can drag or tap the blocks in order; **Check** explains the answer. Ask why Moon needs quotes.
3. **Create (8 min):** let the child change the words in the editor and press **Run Python**. The character says the new words. Invite the child to choose a character and scene, then try another message.
4. **Close (4 min):** ask “What is an instruction?” and “What did an error teach us?” Note whether the child worked independently, with a hint, or wants to practise again.

If the child is excited, continue to Quest 2 with an imaginary character name. Quest 3 can wait until another day. Progress stays in the same browser. The language menu can switch between Portuguese, English, and Spanish; saved stars remain.

## Help without taking over

- Wait a little before intervening. Ask “What did you expect?” and “Which line could we change?”
- Offer one hint at a time. If needed, do the first step together and return the keyboard.
- If the editor feels difficult, begin with the visual activity. Ask what each block does, then compare it with the worked example.
- Celebrate explanations and curiosity rather than speed.
- Ask for one original change after success: a planet name, another number, or a new quiz question.
